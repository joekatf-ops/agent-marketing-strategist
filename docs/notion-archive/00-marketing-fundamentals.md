# What marketing is

Marketing is the system for understanding a market, creating and communicating value, generating demand, converting demand into customers and retaining those customers profitably.

It connects five things:

1. A market with an existing desire, problem or job to be done.
2. A product capable of creating meaningful value.
3. A position and offer that make the value easy to understand.
4. Distribution that puts the message in front of the right people.
5. Economics and measurement that allow the system to continue.

> Market x Product x Message x Distribution x Economics = Marketing System

Creative is one part of marketing. Advertising distributes creative. Sales and conversion turn interest into revenue. Brand builds memory, trust and preference over time.

# Marketing, brand, advertising and sales

## Marketing

Chooses the market, customer, value proposition, positioning, offer, message, channels and measurement system.

## Brand

Creates recognisable meaning, memory, trust and preference. Brand answers: who are we, what do we stand for and why should people remember or believe us?

## Advertising

Pays to distribute a message to an audience. Advertising is a channel for marketing strategy, not the strategy itself.

## Creative

Turns strategy into something people can see, hear and respond to. Creative carries customer, message and relevance signals.

## Sales and conversion

Help a customer make the decision. For ecommerce, this includes the ad, landing page, product page, offer, checkout and follow-up.

Strong growth requires these parts to agree. Great ads cannot permanently rescue a weak product, confusing offer or broken customer experience.

# 1. Start with the market

A market is a group of people who share a meaningful problem, desire or job and have the ability and willingness to act.

## Demand

Marketing rarely invents a deep human desire from nothing. It identifies, focuses and channels existing demand toward a product.

Useful demand questions:

- How painful, valuable or urgent is the problem?
- How many reachable people experience it?
- Are they already looking for solutions?
- Can they afford the product?
- What happens if they do nothing?
- Is the market growing, stable or declining?

## Market selection

A smaller urgent market can be more valuable than a large indifferent one. Before improving copy, confirm that the message is pointed at a problem people care enough to solve.

# 2. Understand the customer

Customers do not buy demographics. They buy progress in a situation.

## Segmentation

Segmentation divides the market into groups with meaningfully different needs, behaviours, situations or purchase criteria.

Useful segmentation lenses:

- Identity
- Situation or life stage
- Behaviour
- Motivation
- Experience
- Problem severity
- Desired progress
- Category awareness
- Purchase criteria

Age, gender and location can help describe a group, but should not replace the behavioural reason they care.

## The Who

In this creative system, Who means the recognisable person or broad segment being addressed. It should be specific enough to guide the message and broad enough to allow multiple executions.

## Jobs to Be Done

A customer hires a product to make progress:

- Functional job: what needs to become easier, faster, safer or better?
- Emotional job: how do they want to feel?
- Social job: how do they want to be seen?

Useful form:

When [situation], I want to [motivation], so I can [desired progress].

# 3. Define the problem and desired progress

A useful problem can contain several layers:

- Symptom: what the customer notices
- Functional problem: what they cannot do
- Emotional problem: how the situation feels
- Deeper problem: why it may continue
- Failed alternative: what they tried and why it disappointed
- Consequence: the cost of leaving it unresolved
- Desired progress: what better looks like

One message should lead with one dominant problem or desired progress. Supporting details may deepen it but should not create competing premises.

# 4. Product and product-market fit

## Product

The product is the mechanism through which value is delivered. Marketing should begin with product truth:

- What does it actually do?
- For whom does it work best?
- What makes it meaningfully different?
- What evidence supports the claims?
- What limitations or trade-offs exist?
- What does the customer experience after purchasing?

## Product-market fit

Product-market fit exists when a defined market repeatedly chooses, uses and values the product enough to sustain growth.

Signals include:

- Consistent conversion
- Repeat purchase or continued use
- Strong customer retention
- Organic recommendation
- Clear customer language about value
- Willingness to pay
- Improving acquisition economics as the system matures

Advertising can reveal or accelerate product-market fit. It cannot manufacture it permanently.

# 5. Positioning

Positioning is the context that makes the products value easy to understand.

It answers:

- What would the customer use or do instead?
- What is different about this product?
- Why does that difference matter?
- Who cares most about that value?
- What category or frame helps them understand it fastest?

The real competitor may be another product, a cheaper substitute, a workaround, the status quo or doing nothing.

Strong positioning makes the product feel like the logical choice for a specific person and problem.

# 6. Value proposition

A value proposition is the clear reason the right customer should choose the product.

It brings together:

- Who it is for
- The important problem or desired progress
- The result the product helps create
- The reason it works or is different
- The proof that makes the promise believable
- The trade-off, effort, delay or risk it reduces

A useful value proposition is clear, relevant, differentiated, believable and economically valuable.

# 7. The offer

The product is what is sold. The offer is the complete value exchange and the reason to act under the stated terms.

Offer components may include:

- Core product
- Quantity or bundle
- Price
- Bonus or gift
- Guarantee or trial
- Shipping and access
- Payment terms
- Credible scarcity or urgency
- Clear CTA

Offer strength comes from increasing the desired value and confidence while reducing delay, effort, uncertainty and risk.

Never use false urgency, invented scarcity or misleading guarantees.

# 8. Pricing and unit economics

Marketing must operate inside commercial reality.

## Core terms

- Revenue: total sales generated.
- AOV: average order value.
- Gross margin: revenue remaining after direct product costs.
- Contribution margin: revenue remaining after variable costs, including advertising where defined.
- CAC: total acquisition cost divided by new customers acquired.
- Break-even CAC: the maximum CAC the business can pay before the defined contribution becomes zero.
- ROAS: attributed revenue divided by advertising spend.
- MER: total business revenue divided by total advertising spend.
- LTV: the value expected from a customer over the relationship.
- Payback period: how long it takes to recover acquisition cost.

ROAS alone does not tell the full story. A business can have attractive ROAS and weak contribution, or lower initial ROAS with strong retention and LTV.

The correct target depends on margin, cash flow, repeat purchase, inventory, growth goals and risk tolerance.

# 9. Awareness

Awareness describes what the customer knows about the problem, available solutions and the product.

## Unaware

They experience a situation but do not recognise the underlying problem.

## Problem Aware

They understand the problem but do not know what can solve it.

## Solution Aware

They know solutions exist and compare approaches.

## Product Aware

They know the product but remain undecided.

## Most Aware

They understand and want the product and mainly need reassurance, an offer or a reason to act.

In this operating system, ads cover the first four levels. Most Aware is handled through the page, product, offer and conversion environment.

Awareness is a messaging state, not a guaranteed funnel sequence.

# 10. Market sophistication

Sophistication describes how familiar and sceptical the market is after seeing similar claims.

A simple promise may work in a new category. A mature category may require:

- Greater specificity
- A clearer mechanism
- Stronger proof
- A fresh comparison
- A new belief or reframe
- Identity or situation-led communication

Awareness asks what the customer knows. Sophistication asks what they have already heard and stopped believing.

# 11. Beliefs and objections

A purchase requires enough necessary beliefs to be true at the same time.

Common belief areas:

- The problem matters
- The desired result is possible
- This category can help
- The mechanism makes sense
- This product can deliver
- It can work for someone like me
- The effort is manageable
- The brand is trustworthy
- The value exceeds the price
- Acting now is better than waiting
- The risk is acceptable

The dominant objection is often the weakest necessary belief. Strong messaging resolves the most important belief gap instead of answering every possible objection at once.

# 12. Funnel and customer journey

A funnel is a measurement model describing movement from attention to purchase and beyond. It is not always a clean sequence.

Typical stages:

1. Attention
2. Interest and recognition
3. Consideration
4. Evaluation
5. Purchase
6. Product experience
7. Retention
8. Advocacy

Meta may show any ad to any eligible person. People enter with different knowledge and may skip stages. Every ad and page must therefore work independently while supporting the wider journey.

# 13. Messaging

Messaging turns customer and product truth into a persuasive argument.

A complete message commonly includes:

- The person or situation
- The important problem
- The desired progress
- The explanation or messaging route
- The product and value proposition
- Proof
- Offer
- CTA

## One dominant idea

One execution should lead with one dominant idea. Extra benefits, features and proof should support that idea rather than compete with it.

## Messaging route

A messaging route is the argument used to communicate the same Who x Problem coordinate. Examples include:

- How it works
- The reframe
- Versus the old way
- Proof that can be seen
- Objection resolution
- Story or belief shift

The route is an execution choice, not a third concept pillar.

# 14. Creative

Creative is the packaged expression of the message.

Creative choices include:

- Awareness entry point
- Hook
- Body structure
- Format
- Creator or authority source
- Visual language
- Proof
- Offer presentation
- CTA
- Destination

Creative diversity means meaningful differences in customer, problem, message, proof or execution. Cosmetic changes alone do not create useful strategic diversity.

# 15. Brand and direct response

Brand and direct response are not opposites.

## Brand contribution

- Builds memory and recognition
- Creates trust
- Shapes category meaning
- Signals identity and values
- Increases the likelihood of future choice

## Direct-response contribution

- Makes the problem and value clear
- Creates a measurable action
- Presents proof and offer
- Reduces decision friction
- Produces immediate market feedback

Strong performance marketing converts while strengthening the meaning the brand wants to retain.

# 16. Distribution and channels

A channel is how the message reaches the market.

Examples:

- Paid social
- Search
- Organic social
- Email and SMS
- Influencers and affiliates
- Retail
- Partnerships
- PR and earned media
- Referral

A channel does not repair a weak market, product or message. Channel strategy considers:

- Where the customer already spends attention
- Customer intent
- Cost and competition
- Creative requirements
- Measurement reliability
- Scalability and concentration risk

# 17. Landing pages and conversion

The ad and destination form one conversion argument.

Maintain message match in:

- Who and situation
- Primary Problem
- Promise
- Mechanism
- Proof
- Offer
- Language
- CTA

## Conversion-rate optimisation

CRO improves the percentage of visitors who take the desired action.

Common conversion levers:

- Clear value proposition
- Relevant page structure
- Strong product explanation
- Credible proof
- Objection handling
- Offer clarity
- Low-friction navigation and checkout
- Page speed and mobile usability
- Trust, shipping, returns and guarantee information

Optimising conversion does not mean manipulating the customer. It means making value and decisions easier to understand.

# 18. Measurement

Measurement should explain the whole system, not reward one platform for every sale.

## Business level

Revenue, contribution, new customers, CAC, MER, cash flow and inventory.

## Funnel level

Impressions, outbound CTR, landing-page views, conversion rate, add-to-cart and checkout.

## Creative level

Spend distribution, hook retention, watch behaviour, comments, messaging route, format and proof.

## Retention level

Repeat purchase, churn, LTV, time between purchases, refund rate, review quality and referral.

Metrics are signals. A metric matters only when it changes a decision.

# 19. Testing and learning

A useful test begins with:

1. A clear hypothesis
2. A defined unit being tested
3. A budget and observation window
4. A success standard
5. A decision rule
6. A retained learning

Early creative testing makes large swings to discover useful territory. Later testing narrows variables to diagnose and improve.

Correlation is not causation. When awareness, format and destination change together, the result identifies the strongest complete execution. It does not prove which isolated variable caused the result.

# 20. Retention and customer value

Acquisition is only the beginning of marketing.

Retention grows when:

- The product delivers the promised value
- Onboarding makes success easier
- Communication supports product use
- Replenishment or repeat purchase is timely
- Service resolves friction
- New products solve adjacent needs
- Customers feel understood and valued

Retention improves LTV, payback and the amount the business can sustainably invest in acquisition.

# 21. Ethics, evidence and trust

Long-term marketing compounds trust.

Required principles:

- Claims match evidence
- Testimonials are real and permitted
- Comparisons are fair
- Urgency and scarcity are genuine
- Pricing and terms are clear
- Customer data is handled responsibly
- The page fulfils the promise made by the ad
- Short-term conversion does not justify long-term deception

# The complete marketing loop

1. Select a valuable market.
2. Understand the customer and problem.
3. Build a product that creates real value.
4. Position it against meaningful alternatives.
5. Create a clear value proposition and offer.
6. Express the message through brand and creative.
7. Distribute it through appropriate channels.
8. Convert demand through a congruent customer journey.
9. Deliver the promised product experience.
10. Measure economics, behaviour and retention.
11. Test, learn and improve.
12. Retain the learning as an organisational asset.
