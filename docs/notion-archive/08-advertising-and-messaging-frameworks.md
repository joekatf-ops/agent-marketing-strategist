# Purpose

Frameworks are thinking tools used after the Who x Problem coordinate is clear. They help choose the best route into the message. They do not add pillars to the concept.

# Framework hierarchy

## Market force

Question: what desire, fear or progress already exists?

Useful lenses: mass desire, Jobs to Be Done, identity, situation and motivation.

## Customer state

Question: what does the person already know and believe?

Useful lenses: awareness, market sophistication, necessary beliefs and objections.

## Messaging route

Question: what persuasive argument should this execution make?

Useful routes:

- How it works
- The reframe
- Versus the old way
- Proof that can be seen
- Objection resolution
- Mechanism or demonstration
- Story or belief shift

## Lead

Question: how should the message enter?

Useful leads:

- Promise
- Problem-solution
- Story
- Curiosity
- Unexpected cause
- Comparison
- Proof
- Offer

## Body structure

Question: in what order should the argument unfold?

Useful structures:

- PAS: Problem, Agitate, Solution
- AIDA: Attention, Interest, Desire, Action
- BAB: Before, After, Bridge
- 4P: Promise, Picture, Proof, Push
- Hook, Story, Offer
- Objection, Reframe, Proof

## Persuasion device

Question: what strengthens belief or action?

Useful devices:

- Demonstration
- Social proof
- Authority
- Specificity
- Risk reversal
- Contrast
- Credible urgency or scarcity

## Expression

Question: how is the idea made immediate?

Useful tools:

- Visual hook
- Spoken or written hook
- On-screen anchor
- Voice of Customer
- Product demonstration
- Story or creator performance

# Selector by awareness

## Unaware

Prioritise recognition, identity, story, curiosity and unexpected cause.

## Problem Aware

Prioritise precise problem language, diagnosis, consequence, failed alternatives and education.

## Solution Aware

Prioritise mechanism, category explanation, comparison, demonstration and proof.

## Product Aware

Prioritise differentiation, objection resolution, testimonials, guarantee, value and offer.

# Message-match rule

The ad and destination form one conversion argument. Maintain continuity in:

- Who and situation
- Primary Problem
- Promise
- Mechanism
- Proof
- Offer
- Language and CTA

# Guardrails

- Sell one dominant idea.
- Specific beats general.
- Every claim creates a proof burden.
- Use frameworks as lenses, not fill-in-the-blank scripts.
- Do not add angle back into the concept equation.
- Do not invent proof, urgency, testimonials or comparisons.
- The strongest framework is the one that solves the current messaging job with the least unnecessary complexity.
