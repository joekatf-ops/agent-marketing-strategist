# The source-of-truth model

> Who We Are Talking To x What Their Problem Is = One Concept Coordinate

Every concept begins with only two decisions:

1. Who are we talking to?
2. What is their primary problem?

Awareness is not a third pillar. Format is not a pillar. A messaging route is not a pillar. These choices determine how the coordinate is expressed at the individual-ad level.

# Pillar 1: Who we are talking to

Define a recognisable person or broad segment. Use only the lenses that make the audience meaningfully clearer.

## Identity

Who are they, or how do they see themselves?

## Situation

What moment, circumstance or stage are they currently in?

## Behaviour

What are they doing now?

## Motivation

What matters most to them right now?

## Experience

What have they already tried or experienced?

Guiding rule: describe a recognisable version of the person, not an overbuilt avatar. Include only details that change the message or creative direction.

# Pillar 2: What their problem is

Choose one primary problem, frustration, tension or unmet desire.

## Symptom

What are they noticing or experiencing?

## Functional problem

What can they not do, or what has become unnecessarily difficult?

## Emotional problem

How does the situation make them feel?

## Deeper problem

What may be causing the visible problem to continue?

## Failed alternative

What have they already tried, and why has it failed them?

## Consequence

What happens if the problem continues?

Guiding rule: supporting details can add depth, but they must not introduce a second competing premise.

# Concept coordinate versus concept test

- Concept coordinate: the enduring Who x Problem pairing.
- Concept test: a specific batch of four ads launched against that coordinate.
- Test ID: the sequential CONTST### code assigned to that batch.
- Source: NNT, INSPO or ITR.

An ITR may keep the same coordinate while receiving a new CONTST number because it is a new test batch.

# Messaging route

A messaging route is the persuasive argument used to communicate the same Who x Problem coordinate inside an execution.

Common routes include:

- A reframe or unexpected explanation
- How the solution works
- A comparison with the old way
- Proof that can be seen
- Objection resolution
- A mechanism, demonstration or belief shift

Changing the route alone does not create a new coordinate. If the Who or primary Problem changes, the coordinate changes.

# Fixed and variable elements

## Fixed within the coordinate

- Who
- Primary Problem

## Variable at execution level

- Awareness
- Messaging route
- Hook and structure
- Media type and format
- Proof and offer
- Visual execution
- Destination

# Framework guardrails

- One Who x one primary Problem per coordinate.
- Do not combine multiple audience premises.
- Do not combine multiple primary problems.
- Awareness changes the entry point, not the coordinate.
- Format is selected after the message strategy is clear.
- Every execution must be independently understandable.
- Evaluate a complete execution before making narrower causal claims.
