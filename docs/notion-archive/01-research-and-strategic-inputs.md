# Purpose

Creative development begins with evidence, not an empty brainstorm. This page defines the inputs required before a concept test is approved.

# The four input layers

## 1. Business economics

Establish the commercial boundaries of the test:

- Target CAC and break-even CAC
- Average order value, margin and contribution after advertising
- Product price, offer and purchase frequency
- Available inventory and fulfilment constraints
- Region, test budget and acceptable learning cost

These inputs determine how much can be spent and what a useful result looks like. They do not determine the concept itself.

## 2. Product truth

Document what can be communicated accurately:

- Product function and real customer use cases
- Materials, design, mechanism and differentiators
- Approved claims and the evidence supporting them
- Required qualifiers and prohibited extrapolations
- Demonstrations, proof assets, reviews and guarantees

A compelling message never justifies an unsupported claim.

## 3. Customer evidence

Use first-party and market evidence to identify recognisable people and important problems:

- Customer interviews and sales calls
- Reviews, support tickets and survey responses
- Search behaviour and community language
- Product usage, demonstrations and observed behaviour
- Previous ad, page and purchase data

Research should reveal how people describe their situation, symptoms, frustrations, failed alternatives, desired progress, objections and proof requirements.

## 4. Market evidence

Study competitors, adjacent categories and other industries to understand:

- Repeated messages and category conventions
- Common formats, structures and proof patterns
- Market sophistication and message fatigue
- White space and underused customer problems
- External patterns that can inform INSPO tests

A long-running competitor ad is evidence of a pattern worth investigating, not proof that it is profitable. Model the underlying structure rather than copying the brand, claim or message.

# Required output

Research should produce:

1. A prioritised list of possible Who definitions.
2. A prioritised list of primary Problems.
3. Evidence supporting each possible pairing.
4. Commercial and claim constraints.
5. Open questions that creative testing needs to answer.

# Guardrails

- Do not turn research into an overbuilt avatar.
- Include only details that change the message or creative direction.
- Keep the core documentation brand-neutral.
- Use named brands, including [brand name redacted], only inside clearly labelled examples.
- Allow real execution data to replace assumptions.
