# Start here

A plain guide to using this thing, written for someone who does not want to know how it works.

You talk to it in ordinary sentences. It does the fiddly parts. Every sentence in a grey box below
is something you can type word for word.

There are four things you will want: attach a brand, get copy written, get an image ad made, and get
research done. That is the whole guide.

---

## First, the one idea you need

This is one strategist that works for **any** brand. It does not know your brand until you attach
one. Attaching a brand means putting that brand's facts in a folder, once, so it stops guessing.

Two rules follow from that, and they explain most of what you will see it do.

1. **It will not make anything up.** If it does not know your price, it writes `[PRICE: confirm]`
   rather than inventing a number. That is deliberate. A made-up review count in a live ad is a
   much worse problem than a gap you can fill in.
2. **Facts never travel between brands.** What it learns about one brand is invisible when it works
   on another. This is on purpose and it is not something to work around.

You will get usable work from a standing start. You will get noticeably better work once a brand is
properly attached, and the gap between the two is large.

## 1. Attaching a brand

### If your store is connected

Easiest path. Type this:

> Attach my Shopify store as a new brand. Pull the products and set the brand folder up for me.

It will build the folder and then tell you honestly what it still does not know. Expect that list to
be long, because a store listing tells it what you sell and what it costs and almost nothing else.

### If your store is not connected

Type this and then just talk normally:

> Set up a new brand called [name]. I will tell you about it and you write it down in the right places.

Then describe the brand the way you would to a new employee. What you sell, who buys it, what they
complain about, what you are and are not allowed to claim. It sorts out where each piece goes.

### Checking where you are

> Is this brand ready for me to ask for copy yet? What is still missing?

You will get a straight answer: `ready`, `nearly` or `thin`, plus what is absent and why it matters.
Nothing is ever blocked. `thin` means the work comes back with more gaps marked in it.

### The shortest honest list of what a brand needs

**Needed before it can write well.** Four things, and only four.

| What | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| What you sell and what it costs | Otherwise every price and product detail is a gap marker |
| Which country you sell in | What you may legally claim changes by country |
| Who buys it and what they complain about | This is the entire basis of the strategy. Without it there is no angle, only description |
| What you are allowed to claim, and what you must never say | Decides whether an ad can actually run |

**Makes it markedly better.** Add these next, roughly in this order.

| What | What it buys you |
|---|---|
| How your brand sounds, with a few lines you like and a few you hate | Stops the copy sounding like everyone else's |
| Proof: review counts, ratings, real customer quotes, guarantees | The difference between an argument and an assertion |
| Your current offer and shipping terms | Needed for the closing line of any ad |
| Actual sentences customers use, copied from reviews | The single biggest source of specificity. Worth more than anything else on this list |
| Your website address | Cheapest way for it to check your own product facts |

**Only needed for particular jobs.**

| What | Needed for |
|---|---|
| Real photographs of your product | Image ads. Not optional for those. See below |
| Your colours, fonts, and what you never want shown | Image ads |
| Your margins and what a customer is worth | Advice about budgets and offers |

### What connecting your store does and does not get you

Worth being clear, because it looks like more than it is.

**It gets you:** every product, every price, every variant, and which market and currency you sell
in. That is two of the four things on the required list, and it takes seconds.

**It does not get you:** anything about your customers, your voice, your proof, or what you are
allowed to claim. And there is one trap worth understanding. Your product page description is
**your own marketing copy**, not evidence. If your page says a product "supports deeper sleep", that
sentence being on your website does not make it an approved claim you can put in an ad. The
strategist deliberately refuses to reuse it, and records it separately as "what the store says".

That refusal is the single most valuable thing it does for you, and it will look unhelpful the first
time you see it.

## 2. Getting copy written

Say what you want and who it is for. You do not need any special format.

> Write me hook options for [product], aimed at someone who does not think they have a problem yet.

> Write Meta primary text and five headlines for [product].

> Write me a 30 second video script for [product].

Useful things to add if you know them:

> ...aimed at people who already use something like this and are not happy with it.

> ...for people who have never heard of this kind of product.

That second one is the hardest and most valuable case, and it will deliberately not lead with your
product name or your price, because to someone who has never heard of you those are not interesting
yet. If that looks wrong to you, ask:

> Why did you not lead with the product?

### Getting it to tell you the truth about its own work

This is the most underused sentence here:

> Now mark your own work honestly. Where does this fall short, and what would fix it fastest?

You will get a specific list. Usually the answer is not "rewrite the copy", it is "approve two
claims and write half a page about how the brand sounds". Believe it when it says that.

### If you do not like something

Paste your edit back:

> Here is how I actually want that line to read: [your version]

> Remember this for next time: we never say [thing].

## 3. Getting an image ad made

> Make me a static image ad for [product], for Facebook and Instagram feed.

It writes a plan first, then generates the picture. You will get a 4:5 upright image, which is the
shape that performs best in feed.

**One thing to do first, and it matters more than anything else you can do here.** Give it real
photographs of your product:

> Here are real photos of the product, use them as the reference.

Here is why, from an actual test run in building this. Two images were generated in the same session
with the same settings. One had a real product photograph attached: the packaging came back
pixel-accurate, wordmark and all. One did not: it produced a nice-looking photograph **of the wrong
product**, an ankle sock where the product is a calf-length sock. It was rejected.

An image generator is excellent at light and mood and unreliable at your product. A real photo fixes
that completely.

**Things it will refuse, and will not be argued out of.** No before-and-after body pictures. No
invented review screenshots, award badges or press logos. No picture that implies a result you are
not allowed to claim in words. The last one catches people out: if you cannot write "sleep better",
you also cannot show a person sleeping peacefully. A picture is a claim.

To see the plan before spending anything:

> Show me the plan and the image description first, before you generate anything.

## 4. Getting research done

> Find out what competitors in [category] are actually running on Facebook right now.

> What do customers complain about in [category]?

> Look at my website and tell me what is out of date.

Real example of what comes back, from a genuine search run while writing this. In the hiking sock
category, a competitor is running this line:

> Cotton socks: soggy by lunch. Merino socks: overheated by 2pm. Alpaca socks: bone dry at 6pm.

Which tells you something no amount of thinking would: merino, which most brands in that category
treat as their selling point, is now being sold against as the old option. That changes what a
merino brand should lead with. That is the kind of thing research is for.

Two honest limits. It can tell you what competitors are **running** and roughly what they are
**spending**. It cannot tell you what is **working**, because nobody publishes that. And a
long-running ad is decent evidence something works, not proof.

## 5. If you want to spend money on ads

It will write you a full launch plan: campaigns, ad sets, budgets, names, what to watch.

> Write me a manual launch plan for [product].

**It will never touch your ad account.** It cannot publish an ad or change a budget. You get
instructions and you press the buttons. This is deliberate and not a limitation to be fixed.

Two numbers it will hold you to: at least 50 dollars a day per ad set, and five full days before you
judge anything. It will tell you a four-day read is too early, because it is.

---

## 6. When something looks wrong

> That does not sound like us. Here is what our brand actually sounds like: [describe it]

> You marked a lot of gaps. Which two should I fill in first?

> I think you have the audience wrong. It is actually [who].

> Are you sure you are allowed to say that?

That last one is always worth asking before an ad goes live. It should tell you exactly which
claims need approving and which are safe.

---

## The one-paragraph version

Attach a brand once, properly, and the difference in output quality is larger than anything else you
can change. Give it real product photographs before asking for image ads. When it marks a gap
instead of writing a number, that is it working correctly. And when you ask it to criticise its own
work, it will tell you the truth, which is usually that the folder needs facts rather than the copy
needing a rewrite.
