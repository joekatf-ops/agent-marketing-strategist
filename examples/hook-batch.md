# Frozen Example: Hook Batch

## 1. Batch header

- Brand: Fieldnote Carry
- Market: Australia
- Product: SnapGrid Cable Pouch
- Concept: FC-CONCEPT-004, "See the cable you need before opening every pocket"
- Awareness: Problem aware and Solution aware
- Destination: product detail page
- Constraints: tabletop, hands, phone camera, product, common cables; no creator required

## 2. Evidence and claim gate

- Verified product truth: transparent mesh divider and six elastic loops, product specification
- Market evidence: 21 of 63 reviewed competitor comments mention digging, tangles, or forgotten cables
- Approved promise: "See your cables at a glance"
- Prohibited: waterproof, indestructible, fits every charger, customer preference claims

## 3. Hook packages

### Hook 1

1. **Category:** Evidence-led safe
2. **Format:** Demonstration
3. **Awareness and belief:** Solution aware; organisation should reduce search friction
4. **Visual opening:** 0.0s closed pouch; 0.5s unzip; 1.2s overhead reveal; 2.0s hand selects one cable
5. **Spoken opening:** "The cable you need, before the digging starts."
6. **On-screen anchor:** "See it. Grab it."
7. **Body handoff:** Show the mesh divider and six loops while naming only verified features
8. **Evidence burden:** Product specification and live visual demonstration
9. **Production:** Product, six cables, overhead phone rig, neutral desk
10. **Risk:** LOW; visible and verified
11. **Fit and difference:** Proves the concept immediately through product action
**Scores:** Fit 5, stop 4, clarity 5, proof 5, brand 5, feasibility 5, learning 4

### Hook 2

1. **Category:** Evidence-led safe
2. **Format:** Comparison
3. **Awareness and belief:** Problem aware; the old storage method creates avoidable searching
4. **Visual opening:** Split screen, loose pouch search on left and one-step selection on right
5. **Spoken opening:** "Same six cables. Two very different mornings."
6. **On-screen anchor:** "Digging vs seeing"
7. **Body handoff:** Repeat both actions under the same conditions, then reveal the divider and loops
8. **Evidence burden:** Fair same-item demonstration, no speed statistic
9. **Production:** SnapGrid, plain pouch, identical cable sets, locked camera
10. **Risk:** LOW; fair comparison with no superiority statistic
11. **Fit and difference:** Enters through contrast rather than a solo product demo
**Scores:** Fit 5, stop 5, clarity 5, proof 5, brand 4, feasibility 4, learning 5

### Hook 3

1. **Category:** Proven pattern
2. **Format:** Confession
3. **Awareness and belief:** Problem aware; the friction is familiar and worth solving
4. **Visual opening:** Hand empties a tangled cable pouch onto a desk, then pauses
5. **Spoken opening:** "I packed a cable pouch and still couldn't find the cable."
6. **On-screen anchor:** "Packed is not organised"
7. **Body handoff:** Repack the same items into visible, fixed positions
8. **Evidence burden:** Market-language pattern and truthful staged situation, not a customer quote
9. **Production:** Hands, two pouches, cable set, desk
10. **Risk:** LOW; actor experience must not be presented as a customer testimonial
11. **Fit and difference:** Opens through an honest failure instead of product proof
**Scores:** Fit 4, stop 4, clarity 5, proof 3, brand 5, feasibility 5, learning 4

### Hook 4

1. **Category:** Proven pattern
2. **Format:** POV situation
3. **Awareness and belief:** Unaware to Problem aware; the activating moment is recognisable
4. **Visual opening:** Phone battery alert, hand reaches into bag, rapid cut to visible cable selection
5. **Spoken opening:** "POV: your battery is on two percent and your cable has disappeared."
6. **On-screen anchor:** "Not now."
7. **Body handoff:** Reveal the cable was visible and fixed inside SnapGrid
8. **Evidence burden:** Common situation from market evidence; no claim about prevalence
9. **Production:** Phone, bag, product, cable, desk or airport-style bench
10. **Risk:** LOW; no personal-attribute implication
11. **Fit and difference:** Starts with urgency in the use situation rather than storage itself
**Scores:** Fit 4, stop 5, clarity 5, proof 3, brand 4, feasibility 4, learning 5

### Hook 5

1. **Category:** Aggressive inside claim ceiling
2. **Format:** Contrarian statement
3. **Awareness and belief:** Solution aware; more pockets are not automatically better organisation
4. **Visual opening:** Pouch with many closed pockets lands beside the open transparent SnapGrid
5. **Spoken opening:** "More pockets can mean more places to lose the same cable."
6. **On-screen anchor:** "Visibility beats guessing"
7. **Body handoff:** Demonstrate the open divider and fixed loops without universal superiority claims
8. **Evidence burden:** Product truth plus fair visual; wording remains conditional
9. **Production:** SnapGrid and one multi-pocket comparison pouch
10. **Risk:** MEDIUM; comparison must remain fair and conditional
11. **Fit and difference:** Challenges the category's pocket-count convention
**Scores:** Fit 5, stop 5, clarity 4, proof 4, brand 5, feasibility 4, learning 5

### Hook 6

1. **Category:** Experimental wildcard
2. **Format:** Letter or note
3. **Awareness and belief:** Broad; visual order itself can carry the value
4. **Visual opening:** Note reading "A place for the cable you always forget" lifts to reveal that loop
5. **Spoken opening:** None for the first two seconds; zipper sound begins the handoff
6. **On-screen anchor:** The physical note is the anchor
7. **Body handoff:** Label three common cable positions, then close and reopen the pouch
8. **Evidence burden:** Product layout only; do not claim the product prevents forgetting
9. **Production:** Product, cables, handwritten note, overhead rig
10. **Risk:** LOW; the copy describes a designated place, not a guaranteed behaviour change
11. **Fit and difference:** Tests a quiet physical feed object against spoken hooks
**Scores:** Fit 4, stop 4, clarity 4, proof 4, brand 5, feasibility 5, learning 5

## 4. Diversity matrix

| Hook | Category | Format | Lead | Visual pattern | Belief | Evidence | Risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Safe | Demonstration | Product action | Reveal | Visibility works | Product truth | Low |
| 2 | Safe | Comparison | Contrast | Split screen | Old way adds friction | Fair demo | Low |
| 3 | Proven | Confession | Failure | Spill and repack | Packed differs from organised | Market pattern | Low |
| 4 | Proven | POV | Situation | Battery alert | The problem matters in the moment | Market pattern | Low |
| 5 | Aggressive | Contrarian | Reframe | Category contrast | Pocket count is not visibility | Product truth | Medium |
| 6 | Wildcard | Letter | Curiosity | Physical note | A visible place carries value | Product layout | Low |

## 5. Recommended production order

1. Hook 2: strongest isolated comparison and highest learning value.
2. Hook 1: clean product-proof control.
3. Hook 5: distinct category reframe after comparison fairness review.
4. Hook 4: use-situation test with modest production needs.
5. Hook 6: low-cost wildcard with a genuinely different feed object.
6. Hook 3: useful language route, but weaker proof than the demonstrations.
