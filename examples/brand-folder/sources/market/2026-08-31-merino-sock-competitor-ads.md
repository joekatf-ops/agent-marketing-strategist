## The finding that matters

**Merino is being attacked as the incumbent, not sold as the upgrade.** Summit Alpaca Socks, eight
days old, runs this verbatim:

> Cotton socks: soggy by lunch. Merino socks: overheated by 2pm. Alpaca socks: bone dry at 6pm.
> It's not even close.

That is a three-rung ladder with merino placed on the middle rung. The whole point of the
construction is that merino is the thing the reader has already tried.

Meanwhile Danish Endurance, the longest-running and highest-reach ad in the set, is still winning
with the older cotton-versus-merino frame and a specific number:

> Cotton holds sweat. Merino moves it. The 38% Merino blend keeps feet dry for hours longer.

So both frames are live. The category has a stage-two argument still working at scale and a
stage-four argument arriving underneath it.

## Second finding: the afternoon frame is the category's shared language

Three of six ads locate the problem at a time of day rather than in a feeling. "Soggy by lunch."
"Bone dry at 6pm." "Hot feet. Damp fabric. Rubbing at the toes."

Acme's own fixture customer language says "damp by lunchtime". The frame is not distinctive, which
matters: it is the right problem and it will not on its own be the right hook.

## Implications for this brand

Strategist judgement. `[UNSOURCED, strategist judgement]`

- Leading on merino puts Acme on the rung a competitor is actively trying to knock it off. The wool
  is a reason to believe, not a wedge.
- The flat toe seam is the better wedge, because it is a construction fact that a fibre swap cannot
  answer. Halfell's symptom stack already names "rubbing at the toes" as a problem and does not
  claim to have solved it.
- Two competitors lead on guarantee length, ten years and one hundred days, against Acme's thirty
  days. The guarantee is a losing slot for this brand and should not be the hook.
