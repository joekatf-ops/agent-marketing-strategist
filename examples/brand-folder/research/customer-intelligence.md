# Customer intelligence

FIXTURE DATA. Acme Trailworks does not exist, and none of the quotes below are from a real person.
See README.md in this folder.

Status: researched, fixture

Every conclusion below is labelled by evidence class, because the classes are not
interchangeable. Brand-customer evidence describes this brand's buyers. Market evidence describes
the category. A brand assertion is what the business believes. Strategist judgement is inference.

## Who, from brand-customer evidence

Two groups, from the 412 reviews on the Crew.

1. **People who walk a lot outside recreation.** Nurses, teachers, hospitality, trades. They did
   not come looking for a hiking sock. Confidence: moderate. Coverage: roughly a third of reviews
   mention work rather than walking.
2. **Day and multi-day walkers.** Came for merino, stayed for the seam. Confidence: high.

## Primary problem, from brand-customer evidence

The recurring sentence is about the afternoon, not the morning. Feet are fine for hours and then
they are not, and the sock has usually moved. Almost nobody describes this as a problem they were
trying to solve. They describe it as what socks do.

Confidence: high. This is the single most repeated shape in the review set.

## Language customers actually use

Verbatim, from fixture reviews:

- "damp by lunchtime"
- "they bunch under the arch"
- "I stopped noticing my feet, which is the point"
- "the seam thing is real"
- "first sock that fits my stupid wide foot"
- "four days on the Overland and I did not think about my feet once"

Words customers do not use: performance, moisture-wicking, thermoregulation, engineered. The
category writes those words. Buyers do not.

## Awareness distribution, strategist judgement

Mostly Unaware and Problem Aware. The reader has accepted damp afternoon feet as normal, so there
is no active search. Solution Aware traffic exists and is small: people already shopping merino.

Tagged `[UNSOURCED, strategist judgement]`. No search or paid data in this fixture supports it.
