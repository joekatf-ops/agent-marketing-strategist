# Concept coordinates, test batches and executions

## Canonical coordinate

One concept coordinate is exactly:

`Who x Primary Problem`

The coordinate is the enduring strategic identity used to organise creative learning.

| Axis | Definition | Useful lenses | Guardrail |
|---|---|---|---|
| Who | A recognisable person or broad segment whose reality changes the message | identity, situation, behaviour, motivation, experience | Demographics may describe a Who but do not automatically make it useful |
| Primary Problem | One problem, frustration, tension or unmet desire that activates attention | symptom, functional problem, emotional problem, deeper problem, failed alternative, consequence | Choose one primary Problem; supporting lenses deepen it rather than becoming extra axes |

Changing Who or Primary Problem creates a new coordinate. Keep the same coordinate when changing
awareness, messaging route, hook, format, creator, proof, offer presentation, visual execution or
destination. Those are execution variables.

## Coordinate, batch and execution are separate

| Layer | Identity | Changes when | Retained record |
|---|---|---|---|
| Coordinate | Who x Primary Problem | Who or Primary Problem changes | coordinate key and linked test history |
| Concept test batch | One launch against a coordinate | any NNT, INSPO or ITR batch is created | new sequential `CONTST###` |
| Execution | One standalone ad in the batch | awareness, messaging route, hook, format, creator, proof, offer presentation, visual or destination changes | complete ad record linked to its CONTST batch |
| Delivery copy | Options for a selected execution | lead route, primary-text length, headline, description or CTA changes | variants linked to the execution |

An ITR may retain the coordinate and change one or more execution variables, but it still receives
the next `CONTST###`. Never reuse a test ID or hide a new batch behind a version suffix.

## Messaging route

Messaging route is the persuasive argument inside an execution. It is not a concept axis. Useful
routes include how it works, reframe, versus the old way, proof that can be seen, objection
resolution, mechanism, demonstration, comparison, story and belief shift.

The route must advance a coherent argument for the selected Who, Primary Problem and awareness job.
The historical term angle may remain in migration records or general copy theory, but it must not
appear in a new concept equation, concept card, name or register field.

## Source classification

| Code | Meaning | Classification rule |
|---|---|---|
| NNT | Net-New Test | A genuinely new Who or Primary Problem hypothesis |
| INSPO | Inspiration | An external format, hook architecture, demonstration, narrative, proof presentation, offer presentation or messaging route is adapted without copying identity, claims, assets, language or script |
| ITR | Iteration | An evidence-led follow-up retains Who and Primary Problem while changing one or more execution variables |

Every source batch receives a new sequential `CONTST###`. An ITR requires an observed signal and a
cited prior test; it is not a pre-signal wording tweak. Source allocation follows evidence maturity.
There is no fixed NNT, INSPO or ITR percentage as a universal default.

## The initial four-ad test

Every initial NNT or INSPO concept test contains exactly four standalone ads:

| Order | Code | Awareness | Job | Default destination |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | UWA | Unaware | Recognition: make the Who recognise the situation or tension | LP |
| 2 | PRA | Problem Aware | Diagnosis: name and explain the Primary Problem precisely | LP |
| 3 | SLA | Solution Aware | Differentiation: explain why this route differs from alternatives | PDP |
| 4 | PDA | Product Aware | Decision: provide the proof and reason to choose | PDP |

LP means landing page and PDP means product-detail page. A deliberate HP or other destination
exception is allowed only when the execution and destination remain congruent and the exception is
recorded. Each ad must make a complete argument because delivery order is never guaranteed.

Most Aware remains useful customer-awareness theory, but it is not a standard ad. The landing page,
product page, offer and wider conversion environment handle its offer, terms, urgency and final
purchase friction.

Initial tests deliberately make large changes across awareness, messaging route, format and
destination. Their results identify strong complete executions and create hypotheses. They do not
prove that an isolated variable caused the outcome.

## Hook selection and delivery copy

For an approved execution, the six-hook development system is a pre-production option set, not six
launch ads. Build strategically different packages across at least four formats, then select one
coherent opening per execution. Its visual hook, spoken or written hook and on-screen anchor all
communicate the same idea, and the body fulfils that opening.

Each selected execution also receives:

- two strategically distinct lead routes;
- short, medium and long primary text for each route;
- exactly five headlines;
- two descriptions;
- one CTA.

These are delivery options linked to the execution. They do not create new coordinates or test IDs
until they become part of a new launched batch.

## Required execution traceability

Every production artefact carries:

1. CONTST test ID and source classification;
2. Who and Primary Problem;
3. awareness code and job;
4. messaging route and primary hook;
5. media type and execution format;
6. proof and claims required;
7. destination and CTA;
8. people, assets and location required;
9. complete final ad name ending in `POSTIDXXX` before publication.

## Evidence-led operating loop

| Stage | Question | Output |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Business truth | What can the brand afford, fulfil and produce? | Economics and constraints |
| 2. Product truth | What may be said and shown? | Facts, proof, claim ceilings and offers |
| 3. Customer intelligence | Who experiences which primary Problems, in what language? | Prioritised Who definitions, Problems and pairing evidence |
| 4. Market intelligence | What arguments and patterns are already visible? | Sophistication, competitor map and opportunity hypotheses |
| 5. Coordinate hypothesis | Which Who x Primary Problem pairing should be tested? | Coordinate, evidence, expected response and open question |
| 6. Test batch | Which source and four executions will produce a useful read? | New CONTST batch with UWA, PRA, SLA and PDA |
| 7. Production and destination | Can each execution be made and continued after the click? | Hooks, ads, copy, proof, assets and destination handoff |
| 8. Observation and decision | What did the supplied evidence show? | Keep, ITR, stop or scale with explanation confidence |
| 9. Retained learning | What should change next time? | Test record plus separately governed approved revision learning |

Preserve coordinate history even when it is rejected or archived. Preserve each test record even
when an ITR keeps the same coordinate. Keep observed associations separate from causal claims, and
keep test learning separate from the append-only, approval-gated human-revision learning ledger.
